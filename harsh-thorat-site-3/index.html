I've always wanted to know what's actually happening underneath things — that's probably why electrical engineering felt like the right place to start, and why a minor in quantum technology keeps handing me stranger questions than I bargained for. Lately I can't stop poking at communication and cryptography: how a signal actually gets from one place to another, and how much of it can be hidden in plain sight.

PLATYPULSE

one board, three wireless worlds

PLATYPULSE is a unified handheld I've been building with a teammate under SRA-VJTI's Eklavya program — the kind of thing I keep coming back to because it sits exactly where my interests overlap. RF, RFID/NFC, and IR each speak their own language and usually need their own separate tool to work with; PLATYPULSE brings all three into one board so you can capture, analyze, store, and replay signals from a single interface instead of juggling three.

It borrows a fair bit of its spirit from the Flipper Zero, but the point was never to clone it — it was to actually understand what happens when a signal is caught, held, and sent back out again, down at the hardware and firmware level. That's the part that ties back to everything else I care about: communication as a practical, physical thing, and cryptography as the question of what a captured signal can, or can't, be made to reveal.
